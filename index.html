The historical journey

From occupation to a numbered presentation sheet.

The Heydrich Block did not appear in isolation. Follow the sequence of people, events, ceremonies and surviving records that led to the presentation sheets of June 1943.

1939–1941

The Protectorate

Reinhard Heydrich's rise, Prague, and the occupation machinery that forms the historical setting for the object.

27 May 1942

Operation Anthropoid

Gabčík and Kubiš attack Heydrich at the Vychovatelna bend. Photographs, maps and surviving evidence reconstruct the event and its immediate aftermath.

4 June 1942

Death, funeral and commemoration

Heydrich's death was followed by state ceremony, propaganda and a memorial programme whose documentary traces survive in archives and photographic sequences.

The first anniversary

Prague Castle ceremonies and the first-anniversary programme provide the setting in which the serial-numbered presentation sheets were distributed.

What this project is

A wartime object reconstructed from the surviving record.

The Heydrich Block is a book and continuing research project devoted to the individually numbered 1943 memorial presentation sheets.

The research follows the evidence from historical context to the object itself: portrait source, design, manufacture, numbering, distribution, recipients, envelopes, surviving examples and postwar provenance.

Its emphasis is new, documented material: archival discoveries, original photographs, institutional records and surviving objects not previously brought together in one reference.

Images from the research

The period seen through selected surviving photographs.

Selected original photographs and contact sheets introduce the documentary record examined in the book. Some have emerged from more than twenty years of research and appear to be previously unpublished; first-publication status is stated only where it can be established from the surviving record.

Among the material under study are photographs of the 1943 memorial programme, including registration and distribution scenes whose exact activities are being tested against captions, archival records and surviving objects. Where first-publication status can be established, individual photographs will be identified accordingly.
